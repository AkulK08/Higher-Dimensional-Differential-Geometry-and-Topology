# Higher-Dimensional-Differential-Geometry-and-Topology

In this paper, we provide an organized introduction to the study of higher-dimensional differential geometry and topology. We begin by going over the fundamentals of vector fields, tangent and cotangent structures, smooth manifolds, and Riemannian metrics. Next, we demonstrate how curvature concepts can be extended to higher dimensions and how the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern theorem relates them to topology. We then provide an introduction to cobordism and surgery theory in higher dimensions, after which Morse theory is developed as a crucial method for examining manifold topology. Characteristic classes (including the Euler, Chern, and Pontryagin classes) and their function in connecting local and global structure will also be covered. The existence of infinitely many smooth structures on $\mathbb{R}^4$ is one of the many reasons why manifolds of dimension $n \geq 4$ lead to phenomena which do not occur in lower dimensions.  There also exist many connections related to physics; for one, the configuration spaces in general relativity, gauge theory, and string theory are inherently higher-dimensional manifolds. In addition, higher-dimensional manifolds need complex invariants like characteristic classes and cobordism groups, whereas surfaces are categorized by genus. So, as a concluding note, we also provide a brief overview of applications to physics and mathematics, such as the Atiyah-Singer Index Theorem.
